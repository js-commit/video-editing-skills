Rules for choosing the angle, in priority order. "A", "B" and "PiP" are the
angles defined above (their names and descriptions come from the job). If PiP
is not in the allowed angles, use B wherever these rules say PiP.

1. **Show it when it is being shown.** When the speaker is handling,
   pointing at, demonstrating or reading off the thing camera B covers
   (the object on the desk, the screen, the instrument, the room), camera B
   plays. Choose **PiP** by default for those moments: the speaker's face in
   the corner costs nothing and their reactions are part of the story.
2. **Reveals and payoffs are PiP, never B alone.** The first look at
   something, a "ta-da", a before/after: the expression is half the content.
3. **B full frame only when B deserves the whole frame**: a detail the
   viewer must see clearly (a small part, fine print, a close comparison),
   a silent demo of several seconds, or a layout on the desk, while the
   narration is descriptive rather than reactive.
4. **A when the speaker addresses the viewer with nothing to show**: intro
   and outro, opinions, stories, prices and verdicts, transitions ("so
   let's talk about..."), anything abstract. Also A when the speaker holds
   the thing UP to camera A ("as you can see here" while raising it): then
   camera A IS the product shot and camera B shows an empty desk.
5. **Runs, not flicker.** Pick the angle per BEAT (a topic, a demo, a
   story), then write it per segment. Hold an angle for at least the target
   run length given above; a one-second segment takes its neighbours'
   angle unless it is a clear reveal. Consecutive segments about the same
   thing get the same angle.
6. **Camera A is home.** Long B / PiP stretches are fine during a demo, but
   a new topic that opens with abstract talk comes back to A.

Cues in the words: "this", "here", "look", "see", pointing language and
physical verbs (open, press, turn, hold, plug, swipe, pour, play) mean the
thing is in play. Counting, specs, "I think", "for me", stories and prices
mean A. Silent segments (no words) are usually demos: PiP unless the
neighbours say B.

When the words cannot tell whether the speaker is looking at camera A or
down at what camera B covers, mark the segment `"confidence": "low"`: those
segments get frame-sampled afterwards. Guessing confidently is worse than
saying low.
