Why this exists

Cutting retakes is not editing.

27skills, raw take to archive
19specialist agents behind them
0uploads you didn't do yourself
100%plain text you can change

Every video I make starts the same way: twenty minutes of me talking to a camera, saying the good sentences two or three times until one comes out right. Turning that into ten clean minutes was the part I dreaded. It isn't creative. It's finding the take where I didn't trip over a word, again and again, for an hour.

So I handed that part to Claude. It reads the word-level transcript, cuts the retakes, the false starts and the mic checks, and a second pass reads the result back like a viewer would. Every cut is snapped to the waveform, so no word gets clipped and nothing pops. I get a timeline in DaVinci Resolve and a preview to watch.

Then I kept going: b-roll, review notes, Shorts, thumbnails, titles and chapters, even clearing the drive when a project is done. This is the toolkit I edit my own videos with, opened up. You keep the taste calls. Claude does the haircut.

— the maintainer, also behind Video Haircut

Cut

From raw take to a clean first cut.

aroll-draft

Raw talking-head take in, first cut out. Retakes, false starts, mic checks and filler gone, every cut snapped to the waveform so no word gets clipped.

Just say “draft this a-roll”

multicam-anglesResolve Studio

Shot with two cameras? Picks the angle for every cut, adds a face picture-in-picture where you show things, and stops the flicker.

Just say “pick the camera angles”

talking-head-ffmpeg

The whole edit with no editing app: your cut plus b-roll as one JSON recipe that ffmpeg renders.

Just say “edit this with b-roll, no Resolve”

unboxing-cut

A long overhead unboxing becomes a paced, ASMR-style sequence: the box, the knife, the peel, the reveal.

Just say “cut the unboxing”

Fill

B-roll, graphics and the lines you forgot to say.

broll-brick-layerResolve Studio

Looks at your b-roll, matches shots to what you're saying, lays them on the timeline, then a second agent tries to veto every one.

Just say “lay b-roll on this timeline”

broll-library

Every clip you've shot, described and searchable. Harvest the b-roll from finished edits and find it again next year.

Just say “have I ever shot a rainy street?”

motion-graphics

Animated stats, charts, callouts and lower thirds timed to your words, in your colours, rendered with the browser you already have.

Just say “add an animated stat here”

vo-gapfill

Missing a sentence? Writes the line in your on-camera voice and fills the gap with your own text-to-speech voice.

Just say “do a voiceover for that gap”

camera-ab-compareResolve Studio

Two phones, one walk: works out which phone shot what, pairs matching moments and builds the side-by-side.

Just say “compare these two phones side by side”

Polish

Notes, drafts, audio and shaky shots.

edit-review-notes

Watch the draft, type or dictate notes in EditReview as you go, and Claude applies them. The next draft shows only what changed.

Just say “apply my review notes”

resolve-export-draft

A review draft of your Resolve timeline, rendered from the command line. No Deliver page.

Just say “render a draft”

voice-clearApple silicon

Just say “clean up my voice”

stabilize-clips

Smooths shaky handheld clips, measures whether it helped, and keeps the original when it didn't.

Just say “stabilize these clips”

It learns your taste

Say it once.
It sticks.

  • Preferences. "The cuts are too tight" or "always keep my jokes" goes into user/preferences.md, and every skill reads that file first.
  • Your own copy. Ask Claude to change how a skill works, add a step, or write a new one. It edits the files in your folder, and git diff shows exactly what changed.
  • Undo is one sentence. "Put the skill back how it was" works too.

Get started

Rolling in three steps.

Install Claude Code and ffmpeg

Claude Code from Anthropic's setup page, then ffmpeg:

brew install ffmpeg

Clone it and open Claude

Always start Claude from this folder. Your projects can live anywhere.

git clone https://github.com/js-commit/video-editing-skills && cd video-editing-skills && claude

Type /setup

It checks what's installed and asks where your stuff lives. Every question can be skipped.

/setup

Then hand it a take: draft this a-roll: ~/Movies/my-raw-take.mp4

  • A Claude plan with Opus (Pro or Max: setup asks and tunes the agents to it)
  • Python 3.9+ (on a Mac, Apple's Command Line Tools have it)
  • Transcription: ElevenLabs Scribe (free tier) or voz, offline on Apple silicon
  • Optional: DaVinci Resolve (Studio for full automation), a NAS for archives
Your footage, your call

Nothing leaves without saying so.

Files stay put

Your video files stay on your computer. Claude reads the transcripts, text and contact sheets a skill shows it, and skills that look at frames say so.

A private mode

For home videos, a local vision model describes frames on your Mac, so no frame leaves it. Or pick no-frames and nothing looks at a frame at all.

You press upload

Nothing is ever uploaded to YouTube for you. You get a paste sheet and upload in YouTube Studio yourself.

Ask before deleting

None of your footage or projects is deleted, moved or overwritten without your yes. Archiving is a dry run first, always.

Questions

Fair questions.

Is it really free?

The skills are MIT licensed. You need Claude Code with a Claude plan, and its usage counts against that plan: a 15-minute a-roll draft runs a handful of Opus agents. Transcription is ElevenLabs Scribe (free tier, then per minute) or voz, which is free and runs offline on an Apple silicon Mac with macOS 26.

Do I need DaVinci Resolve?

No. The ffmpeg path does the whole edit: cut, b-roll, graphics and the final render. With DaVinci Resolve Studio, Claude builds and changes the timeline for you; the free version works through XML import.

Mac only?

macOS gets the most testing, and the review apps (EditReview, BulkCut) and the on-device audio tools ship signed and notarized for it, with nothing to compile. The ffmpeg paths are written to work on Windows and Linux too.

Will it make my videos sound like AI?

It doesn't write your video. It cuts your own words: mistakes out, everything you meant to say kept, tangents and jokes included. Titles and descriptions are written from what you actually said, and you pick.

I already use Video Haircut.

aroll-draft's cut list and edited transcript open in Video Haircut for manual touch-ups. Say "haircut this video" and it knows what you mean.

Can I change how it edits?

Yes, that's the point. Every skill is a text file in your copy. Tell Claude what you want different and it edits the skill; tell it "from now on..." and it writes it to your preferences.
