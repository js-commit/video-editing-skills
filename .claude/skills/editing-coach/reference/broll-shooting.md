# Shooting good b-roll

B-roll is the footage that shows what you're talking about. Good b-roll is
planned, varied, steady and long enough to edit.

## Plan from the script

- Go through what you'll say and list a shot for each point that would be
  better *shown* than told: the feature, the problem, the result, the place.
- Add a few generic shots for transitions: an establishing wide, hands at
  work, details, the environment.
- Shoot b-roll after the a-roll when you know what you actually said.

## Variety: the five-shot habit

For each subject, get:
1. **Wide**: where we are.
2. **Medium**: the subject and what's happening.
3. **Close-up**: the detail that matters.
4. **Over the shoulder / POV**: the viewer's point of view (hands using it).
5. **Something unusual**: low angle, top-down, through something,
   a reflection.

Cutting between sizes looks intentional; five similar medium shots look
like one long one.

## Make every clip usable

- **Hold each shot 8-10 seconds** before and after the move. Editors need
  handles (spare frames to trim); a 2-second clip is often unusable.
- **One move per shot**, slow and steady: a push-in, a pull-out, a slide, a
  tilt, a turn of the product. Start and end the move still for a second.
- **Steady**: tripod, slider, gimbal, or brace your elbows; turn on the
  camera's stabilization for handheld (off on a tripod). The stabilize-clips
  skill can smooth shaky clips afterwards, at the cost of a slight crop.
- **Clean frame**: tidy the background, wipe fingerprints and dust off
  products, remove stickers and price tags, hide cables.
- **Privacy**: check screens (notifications, messages, email addresses),
  house numbers, documents and car plates in frame before you shoot.

## Camera settings

- Match the a-roll: same frame rate, similar white balance and look, so shots
  cut together.
- **Shutter at about double the frame rate** (1/50 for 24/25 fps, 1/60 for
  30 fps): natural motion blur. Use ND filters outdoors to keep this with a
  wide aperture.
- **Slow motion**: shoot 60 fps (or 120) and play it at 24/30 in the edit.
  Shutter then 1/120 (or 1/250).
- Manual white balance and exposure for each setup, so a move doesn't change
  brightness mid-shot.
- Lock focus or use a focus mode you trust; a focus hunt ruins a shot.

## Light

- Soft light from the side or 45 degrees in front shows shape and texture.
- Shiny products: a big diffused source and a black card to shape
  reflections; avoid bare lamps reflected in screens.
- Backlight (a light behind the subject) separates it from the background.

## Sound

Record camera sound on b-roll anyway: ambient sound (clicks, pours, steps)
under b-roll makes it feel real. Keep it low in the mix.

## Organize as you go

- Copy cards to the project's footage folder the same day.
- Name or tag clips by subject when you can. The broll-library skill can
  describe and index clips so you (and Claude) can search them later.
