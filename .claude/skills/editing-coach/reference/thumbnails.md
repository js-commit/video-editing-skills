# Thumbnails principles

The thumbnail and title together decide whether someone clicks. The
thumbnail has to work at the size of a fingertip on a phone.

## The rules that matter most

1. **One idea.** A thumbnail shows one thing: the result, the problem, the
   comparison, the reaction. If it needs explaining, it's too complicated.
2. **Three elements at most**: e.g. a face, a product, two or three words.
3. **Readable small.** Check it at about 200 pixels wide (the size in a phone
   feed). If text or the subject is unclear there, simplify.
4. **Contrast.** Bright subject on a darker or plain background (or the
   reverse); a thin outline or glow separates a cut-out subject.
5. **Few words, big letters.** Zero to four words, bold font, not a repeat of
   the title: the title and thumbnail should add to each other.
6. **Faces with a clear emotion** draw the eye, when a person fits the video.
   Eyes looking toward the subject or the text lead the viewer there.
7. **Leave the bottom right clear**: YouTube draws the video length there.
8. **Honest.** Show what the video delivers. A misleading thumbnail wins a
   click and loses the viewer in seconds (retention drops, and so does reach).

## Technical

- 1280x720 pixels (16:9), JPG or PNG, under 2 MB (YouTube's limit).
- For Shorts, the frame chosen from the video or a 1080x1920 custom image;
  the center square is what shows in most places.
- Keep a consistent style (colors, font, layout) so returning viewers spot
  your videos.

## Making one

- Shoot for it: take a few sharp photos or grab a frame at the end of the
  shoot with the subject lit well. Video frames work if they're sharp
  (pause on a frame without motion blur).
- Cut the subject out, brighten and saturate it a little more than looks
  natural (thumbnails are viewed small), add the words, check it small.
- Make two or three different ideas, not five versions of one. YouTube
  Studio can test up to three thumbnails against each other (Test & compare,
  in recent versions).
- The youtube-thumbnails skill builds candidates from your real footage and
  checks legibility and privacy.

## Avoid

- Tiny text, lots of text, thin fonts.
- Busy backgrounds and many small objects.
- Screens with private information (notifications, emails, names).
- Logos and brand imagery you don't have permission to use as the main
  element.
